# trajectory_planner.txt — plan a ground trajectory AND describe the scene, first-person.
#
# Tokens (literal {name}, substring-substituted by _fill_prompt — NOT str.format):
#   {description}  the navigation instruction to follow
#   {min_row}      farthest image row (of 1000) a point may occupy (reach cap)
#
# Returns JSON: {"reasoning": <what I see + why this path>,
#                "waypoints": [{"point": [y, x], "label": <n>}, ...]}
# The "reasoning" is the ONLY report of the scene my mission memory ever gets, so it
# must DESCRIBE what is visible from my viewpoint, not merely justify the path.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

I am a mobile robot looking through my forward-facing camera. Plot the path I should drive to
follow this instruction: "{description}".

Place a sequence of at least 10 points on the floor along that path. They must lie ON the
traversable ground in front of me (never on walls, furniture, or obstacles), and honor any
preference in the instruction (which side to keep, what to avoid). Label them in order from "0"
(closest to me, at the bottom of the image) to <n> (the last point). Points are [y, x] normalized
to 0-1000, where y is the image row: 1000 is right in front of me, and smaller y is farther ahead.

Keep the trajectory SHORT and conservative. Place NO point above row {min_row} — every point must
have y >= {min_row}. Stay in the nearer, lower part of the image, where the ground is close and I
can judge it reliably; the farther a point is (smaller y, toward the top), the less accurately I
can place it. If my target is farther than that, do NOT try to reach it in one plan: lay a short
path toward it and stop at the reach limit — I will re-plan and continue as I get closer. The last
point is just how far I go this step, not necessarily the final goal.

In "reasoning", FIRST describe what I actually see around me, THEN why I chose this path:
- the space I'm in (room type and rough layout);
- landmarks and furniture, and where they are relative to me (ahead, to my left, to my right);
- doorways and exits I can see — and where each seems to lead, if I can tell;
- whether my target is actually in view, and if not, what is blocking me from it;
- then one sentence on the path I'm taking (or, if there is none, why).
Report what I SEE, not what I assume or expect.

Return JSON only, no markdown fencing:
{"reasoning": <2-4 sentences: what I see, then my path>, "waypoints": [{"point": [y, x], "label": <n>}, ...]}

Leave "waypoints" empty if there is no valid ground path toward my target — a wall or obstacle is
in the way, or I am already there — and still describe what I see and say why in "reasoning".
